# Facilitator guide — AI FUTURE ME: THE 30-DAY JOURNEY (online)

**Goal:** participants leave with a short, personal, realistic action plan: three small things they will do in the next 30 days to use AI at work, with a day, an AI tool or approach, evidence, and a plan for what could get in the way.
**Format:** individual, 7–9 minutes of play + 4–6 minutes of debrief. (Classroom version: individual, about 5 minutes, then 2–3 volunteers share one step.)

## Know the source before you present
- From the course notes: the goal, “write 3 small, realistic things you will do in the next 30 days to use AI at work — small, not ‘become an AI expert’”, the three examples, 2–3 volunteers share one step, debrief questions 5–6 below, and the tip to keep a photo of the plan. The handout is not in the deck.
- The 7 / 14 / 30-day windows, destinations, tool and evidence choices and the three obstacles are scaffolds written for this game. The manager conversation starter follows a later course slide.
- The game checks **structure** only (an action, a tool, a day, evidence). It cannot judge whether a plan is realistic — you and the participant do that. Use the **WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE** module to coach.

## Before the session (5 min)
- Open the link → Trainer (PIN 2026). One session per group (**+ New session**). Put the **player link** (or the 5-character code) in the chat.
- Tell everyone: **small, realistic actions only — no confidential workplace details** (the plan reaches you over a public relay).

## What happens (for narration)
1. **Destination** — what do you most want AI to help you improve? (lights the start of the path)
2. **Three milestones** — an action, a tool, a day and evidence for each; examples are only a start — copying one word for word doesn’t count.
3. **Obstacles** — not enough time, unreliable output, data restrictions; some tempting answers are risky.
4. **Roadmap check** — are the parts there? Rate each step honestly; shrink anything too big.
5. **Future me** — a planned (not done) roadmap and a message built from your own plan; copy, download, print, calendar reminders, manager conversation.

## What good looks like (also in the dashboard)
- **Small:** one task, one tool, a few minutes. **Specific:** what, with which tool, for whom or how often. **Observable:** something you can point to afterwards. **Scheduled:** a day inside its window, ideally tied to an existing routine. **Safe:** no confidential data in public AI tools; AI output is a draft you check; you stay accountable.
- Coach: “Become an AI expert” (too big) · “Use AI more” (too vague) · “Summarize the real customer file” in a public tool (unsafe).
- Obstacle responses — **safe:** shrink to ten minutes, attach to an existing routine, block a slot; treat output as a first draft and check facts, clearer context, a colleague’s review; non-confidential or made-up examples, the approved tool, anonymize first, check the policy / ask IT. **Risky:** wait for a quieter month, cram on the last day; trust confident output, copy straight into a client email; paste the real document into a public tool, use a personal account for work files.

## Debrief questions
1. *(Course notes)* Which of your 3 steps is the easiest to start tomorrow?
2. *(Course notes)* What is one thing that could stop you from doing this — and how can you plan around it?
3. *(Suggested)* How would you tell your manager? (The 3-point manager conversation is in the plan.)
4. *(Suggested)* Which step would you shrink if you had only ten minutes a week?

## Notes
- **Course tip:** ask everyone to save or print their plan before they leave (Copy, Download, Print and calendar buttons are in the game) — a plan you keep is a plan you use.
- Scoring rewards completeness, honest realism and revision — never speed. Use it for feedback, not for grading people.
- If the live connection drops: **Refresh** on the dashboard rebuilds from the relay history; otherwise collect the downloaded plans and use **Import**. Export CSV at the end if you want everyone’s commitments.
